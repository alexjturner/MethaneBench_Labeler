How to label methane plumes

Please read this short guide before you start. You can reopen it any time with the Instructions button in the top bar.

Your task

Each scene is a small patch of satellite imagery. For every scene, decide whether a methane plume is visible:

  • If you see one or more plumes, draw a polygon around each plume, then click Submit.
  • If there is no plume, click No plume visible, then Submit.

The image views

Click the thumbnails to the right of the main image to switch views. Your polygons stay in place when you switch, so you can check one outline against several views.

RGBTrue-colour image. Use it to see the surface: facilities, roads, fields, water, clouds.
MethaneMethane signal: how unusual this image's shortwave-infrared band ratio (dR) is compared with the same place on other dates that year. Red means more methane-like absorption than usual, which is where plumes show up. Things that changed on the ground (new pads, ponds, wet soil, crops, cloud shadows) can also turn red, so look for a plume shape and check red features against RGB and Hi-res.
NDVIVegetation index (light grey = bare ground, green = vegetation). Use it to check whether a red Methane feature just follows a field or other vegetation edge.
Hi-resHigh-resolution basemap (Esri World Imagery) of the same area, for identifying surface features. Scroll to zoom and drag to pan. Keep in mind:
  • Not from the scene date. The basemap is a mosaic of images from different dates, often years apart from the satellite scene. Pads, ponds, roads and other facilities may have been built, removed or changed since.
  • Approximate position. The scene is on a UTM grid and the basemap on a web map projection, so the scene outline (dashed) and your polygons can be offset by up to a few tens of metres, about one or two image pixels, mostly near the edges.
  • Resolution varies. About 0.3–1 m in most places, coarser in some remote areas. Zooming in past the available detail shows a "Map data not yet available" tile.
  • Context only. Methane cannot be seen in it. Use it to identify surface features (pads, tanks, pits, roads, fields) that could explain a red Methane feature. You cannot draw on this view; your polygons are shown as outlines.
MaskCloud mask from the satellite product, shown only when available. Light areas are flagged as cloud or cloud shadow. Tick Cloud overlay above the image to tint those pixels on every view.

Click the ⓘ button under the image to see how the current layer is calculated.

Drawing polygons

  1. Click on the image to place each corner of the polygon, or click and drag to trace the plume outline freehand. Releasing the mouse closes a freehand outline. With Snap to pixels (on by default), each finished outline is redrawn along the edges of the image pixels it covers.
  2. Double-click, or click the first corner again, to close the polygon.
  3. Draw a separate polygon for each plume if there is more than one.
  4. To trim a polygon, turn on Eraser (above the image, or press E) and outline the part to remove, by clicking or dragging as when drawing. The erased pixels are removed from every visible polygon. Erase from the edge: a hole in the middle of a polygon is not allowed. Press E again to go back to drawing.
  5. Press Esc to cancel an outline you are still drawing, or click Undo to reverse the last change (a new polygon, an erase or a delete).
